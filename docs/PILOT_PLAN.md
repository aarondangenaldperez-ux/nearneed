# NearNeed controlled pilot plan

## Phase 0 — Validate the problem

**Do not advertise a functioning marketplace yet.**

- Interview adults who recently needed a local service.
- Interview adults who currently provide services.
- Ask for the last real example and current workaround.
- Compare those workarounds against social groups, referrals, search engines, and existing marketplaces.
- Choose one locality and two or three categories only if both sides show enough repeated demand.
- Record the decision and the evidence without retaining unnecessary personal information.

## Phase 1 — Concierge pilot

Before building complex automation, manually coordinate a small number of requests with informed participants.

- Obtain clear consent before sharing a request with any provider.
- Share only the minimum request details.
- Never share a customer's exact address or personal contact details in a public feed.
- Keep communications within a documented, moderated channel.
- Do not accept platform payments or promise guarantees.
- Record request quality, response time, provider fit, customer choice, completion, and reports.
- Stop or pause the pilot if a serious safety, privacy, or fraud concern cannot be handled.

Manual coordination must not become an excuse to bypass privacy notice, consent, or basic safety controls.

## Phase 2 — Private beta

Entry criteria:

- Authentication and RLS tests pass.
- A report and moderation workflow exists.
- Terms, privacy notice, community rules, and support process are ready.
- Only the chosen service categories and locality are enabled.
- Provider profiles are clearly labeled; no unearned verification claims.
- Users understand that NearNeed is a matching platform, not the service provider or a guarantee of work quality.

Observe and fix failures before inviting more users.

## Phase 3 — Public launch decision

Proceed only when evidence shows:

- customers repeatedly post valid requests;
- providers respond with relevant offers within a useful time;
- at least some requests are completed and confirmed;
- the rate of reports and unresolved disputes is manageable;
- the team can moderate and support the volume;
- unit economics have a plausible route to sustainability.

Do not expand categories or geography simply because the site can technically support them.

## Metrics

Instrument the funnel without collecting more personal data than needed:

- request started → request published;
- request published → first relevant offer;
- request published → at least one offer;
- offer received → offer accepted;
- accepted offer → completion confirmed;
- repeat customer and provider activity;
- reports, cancellations, disputes, and response time.

Set numeric targets only after establishing a baseline. Separate verified completions from self-reported completions.

## Revenue experiments

Do not monetize the first validation round. Once providers repeatedly receive relevant requests, interview them about the value and cost of leads. Test one transparent paid option at a time, with a free baseline and no guaranteed-work claims. Delay commissions until transaction support, refunds, dispute handling, and any applicable obligations are understood.

## Go / no-go

**Go:** repeat real demand, adequate provider response, and safe operations.

**No-go / revise:** weak repeat demand, empty supply, poor offer relevance, high moderation burden, or customers preferring current alternatives.

A no-go result is useful: it prevents building an expensive product around an unproven assumption.
